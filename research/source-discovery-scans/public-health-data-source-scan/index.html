    Public Health–Related Public Data Sources for a Consumer App in Canada With Ontario Focus¶

    Executive summary¶

    This scan identifies a broad set of public (or publicly viewable) health-related datasets, dashboards, registries, feeds, maps, directories, and reports that could realistically power features in a consumer-facing app in Canada, with special emphasis on Ontario. The most app-usable sources cluster into a few patterns: federal/provincial open-data portals with explicit open licences; government or agency APIs; and municipal open-data portals (often ArcGIS FeatureServer or downloadable CSV/XLSX). citeturn29search16turn9search9turn3search0turn26search2turn30search13

    A recurring constraint is that many high-consumer-interest resources (inspection search portals, outbreak pages, and hospital wait-time clocks) are “publicly viewable” but not reliably reusable: exports/APIs are absent or the reuse terms are unclear, meaning a tool may need either (a) an official open-data source for the same data, (b) a partnership, or © manual curation/scraping with legal review. citeturn39search2turn17search14turn10search15turn12search0turn9search1

    From an Ontario-first perspective, the most promising “buildable” foundations are: Ontario’s open-data datasets for provider/facility locations and performance (e.g., service-provider geospatial locations; ambulance response-time reporting; drug formulary extracts); Public Health Ontario’s interactive surveillance tools where downloads are explicitly offered; and municipal open-data portals in major cities/regions for inspections, beach testing, AEDs, and EMS datasets. citeturn30search13turn13search0turn26search14turn16search13turn25search9

    Caveats and recommended next steps¶

    Several sources are clearly public to view but not clearly licensed or engineered for reuse. Common examples include inspection portals (restaurant/personal service settings/pools) and hospital-operated wait-time pages: they are consumer-facing but often lack official export routes or explicit data-reuse terms on the page itself. citeturn39search2turn39search7turn10search15turn12search0

    Public data is fragmented across Ontario public health units and municipalities, with varying technology stacks and continuity risks (e.g., platform retirement notices), which means an app may need per-jurisdiction connectors and a data normalization layer rather than one “Ontario feed.” citeturn4search20turn4search21turn38search13turn4search23

    Many tools are “public dashboards” but do not confirm downloadable data in the captured primary text; for those, reuse should be treated as uncertain until you confirm an official download button, an API, or published open-data resources for the same underlying dataset. citeturn29search25turn16search4turn33search3turn10search5

    Some high-value directories and registries are explicitly request-gated or partner-only (examples include 211 API access by contact, PHO STI lab tool restricted to authorized public health professionals, and provider-oriented perinatal dashboards). These are often not realistically usable in a consumer app without a formal agreement. citeturn9search1turn16search0turn27search1

    Recommended next steps, constrained to practical build actions: - Treat open-licenced portal datasets as your backbone (federal open portal + Ontario Data Catalogue + municipal portals), then add “view-only” sources only where they fill a critical gap. citeturn29search16turn9search9turn13search1turn38search6 - For “view-only” sources you still want, do an explicit terms-of-use review per site and prefer any official export routes over scraping; where terms are unclear, downgrade usability and/or pursue partnership. citeturn12search0turn9search1turn39search2 - Build a lightweight data catalog layer in your product that stores: provenance (URL), refresh cadence, licence flag, and connector type (API vs file download vs manual), so you can change sources when portals migrate or dashboards change. citeturn4search20turn29search5turn30search13
