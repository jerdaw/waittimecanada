**Status:** Archived after Batch A delivery

## Summary

This brief translates the public-health-data-hub idea into a constrained planning target.

The working decision is:

**If Wait Time Canada expands beyond emergency wait times, it should do so as an Ontario-first, provenance-first public health access and systems data module centered on public, location-aware, operational datasets.**

This is not a decision to build everything that is publicly visible. It is a decision to evaluate only the categories that are:

- public-facing and operationally useful
- legally and technically reusable
- understandable without giving medical advice
- coherent with the current observatory identity

---

## Intended Audiences

### Primary

- Ontario residents looking for practical, public, location-aware health access and safety information

### Secondary

- journalists, students, and policy observers who need source-linked, interpretable public health data

### Tertiary

- future employers evaluating the repo as a health-data, civic-tech, and data-platform project

---

## Problems Worth Solving

The hub track should focus on public-interest questions such as:

- Where are relevant public health access or emergency-support resources near me?
- What official public safety alerts or health product alerts should I know about?
- What public environmental conditions or advisories matter right now?
- What official public health infrastructure exists in a region?

The hub track should **not** focus on:

- private patient-level workflows
- triage or symptom-routing logic
- treatment recommendations
- wellness, quantified-self, or general lifestyle features

---

## Product Boundary

### In Scope

- provider and facility directories
- AED location strategy
- recalls, safety alerts, and health-product reference data
- environmental health overlays
- selected public system context that is interpretable and low-risk

### Explicitly Out of Scope

- private or authenticated clinical data
- medical advice
- partner-only data without a credible access path
- scraping-first foundations for safety-critical live features
- broad “health portal” ambitions without strong narrative fit

---

## Decision Principles

Any candidate domain must satisfy all of the following before it can become Batch A work:

1. It has clear public value.
2. It has a primary-source-verified access path.
3. Its reuse posture is acceptable.
4. Its freshness risk is acceptable for the intended use.
5. It fits the existing Wait Time Canada narrative.

If a candidate domain fails one of those, it can still remain in research, but it does not move into milestone planning.

---

## Success Criteria For This Planning Package

The planning package is only considered complete if it produces:

- a validated shortlist instead of a brainstorm
- a weighted ranking method applied consistently
- a provisional Batch A recommendation
- a recommendation on product identity
- explicit kill criteria and exclusions
- a handoff brief that is ready for Batch A milestone planning

---

## Provisional Planning Outcome

Based on the validated shortlist and scoring matrix, the current recommended first batch is:

1. provider and facility baseline
2. AED source strategy
3. recalls and safety alerts
4. one environmental health overlay

This package should remain **inside Wait Time Canada** for the next planning phase rather than becoming a separate product effort now.

---

## Defaults Chosen

- Ontario-first scope
- planning+research doc split
- shortlist-first validation rather than broad inventory validation
- forced identity recommendation rather than open-ended option framing
- Batch A chosen provisionally now and confirmed in the next milestone-planning pass
